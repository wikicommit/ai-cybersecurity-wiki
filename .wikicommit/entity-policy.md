The landmarks on this map are techniques, systems, frameworks, rules and incidents, not people.
Do not create a page for a person. Name researchers in the body of the page for their work.
Never name a private individual involved in an incident (an affected customer, an employee, a
claimant, a bereaved relative); refer to them by role. A legal case may be titled by its
published case name, but the body refers to the parties by role.

An incident is a page of its own (Event) when a source reports what happened in it. Record, in
the body, when it happened, when it was discovered and when it was disclosed, as separate dates
where the source gives them, and who disclosed it. When the sources differ about what happened,
or a source attributes a claim to one of the parties, say which party says what; do not state
one party's account as fact. When a source says an investigation or a proceeding is still open,
say so and set expires_at to the date the source expects a result, or to three months after the
source's date if it gives none.

An AI model or agent involved in an incident is a SoftwareApplication. A threat-actor group is
an Organization only when a source treats the group as its subject.

A framework or a taxonomy is one page for the framework, with its items listed on it. Give an
item a page of its own only when a source treats that item as its subject (for example, prompt
injection). When a framework has several editions, the framework's page says which edition is
current and what changed between editions, as the sources state.
