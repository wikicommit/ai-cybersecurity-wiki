be published: Creative Commons Attribution (CC BY) or Attribution-ShareAlike (CC BY-SA),
a work of the United States federal government, or the UK Open Government Licence v3.0.
Do not register a source that reserves all rights, forbids adaptation (ND), restricts use
to non-commercial purposes (NC), or whose terms could not be found — even when the page
would only summarise it. For arXiv, read the licence link on the abs page; the arXiv default
licence is not an open licence.

Check the document's own notice as well as the site's terms: a site under an open licence
can host a document under other terms, and images and figures are often excluded.

Take in encyclopaedia articles (Wikipedia), frameworks and guidance published by standards
bodies, security agencies and their projects (OWASP, NIST, NCSC, DARPA, FBI, the European
Commission), and papers under an open licence. Record the terms in source.license at
registration.

Prefer the HTML version of a paper (for arXiv, arxiv.org/html/<id>) over its PDF when both
exist. When a framework or report has several editions, take in each edition as its own source;
do not replace an earlier edition with a later one.

Do not take in news articles, company blogs, incident disclosures by companies, opinion
columns, vendor marketing, product comparisons, repositories of exploit code or attack
prompts, or leaked material. A page may name such a document when a registered source
cites it; it is not registered itself.
