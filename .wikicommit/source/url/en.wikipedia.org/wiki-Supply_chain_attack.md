## Summary

A supply chain attack is a cyber-attack that seeks to damage an organization by targeting less secure elements in its supply chain, either by tampering with hardware or by exploiting the software supply chain, where a low-level component is used to inject malicious code into the larger software that depends on it. The article describes how such attacks typically begin with an advanced persistent threat seeking the weakest member of a supply network, the risks they pose across industries, a series of examples from Target and Stuxnet to SolarWinds, the XZ Utils backdoor, an npm typosquatting campaign, Notepad++ and eScan, and prevention efforts by governments, financial institutions and security firms, including the guidance NIST delivered under Executive Order 14028.
