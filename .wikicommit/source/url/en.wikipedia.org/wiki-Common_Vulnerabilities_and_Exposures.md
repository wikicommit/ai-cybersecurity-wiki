## Summary

Common Vulnerabilities and Exposures (CVE), originally Common Vulnerability Enumeration, is a system launched in 1999 that provides a reference method and common identifiers for publicly known information-security vulnerabilities; it is maintained by an FFRDC operated by the MITRE Corporation with funding from the US Department of Homeland Security, and underlies the US National Vulnerability Database. The article explains how CVE identifiers are assigned by CVE Numbering Authorities, the reserved, published and rejected states of an entry, the database's fields, the 2015 change to variable-length IDs, and criticism of the assignment rules. It also covers the 2025 funding crisis, in which the MITRE contract was extended for 11 months just before it expired, and the program's later move to core funding.
