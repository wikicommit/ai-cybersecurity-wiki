## Summary

Differential privacy (DP) is a mathematical framework for releasing statistical information about a dataset while provably limiting what can be inferred about any individual in it, typically by adding noise calibrated to a query's sensitivity. The article covers the formal (ε, δ) definition introduced in 2006, its composition, post-processing and group-privacy properties, mechanisms such as the Laplace mechanism and randomized response, real-world deployments, and attacks on implementations such as timing and floating-point side channels.
