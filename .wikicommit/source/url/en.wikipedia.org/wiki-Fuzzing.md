## Summary

Fuzzing, or fuzz testing, is an automated software testing technique that feeds a program invalid, unexpected or random inputs and monitors it for crashes, failed assertions or memory leaks; it is used mostly to expose security vulnerabilities. The article traces the term to a 1988 University of Wisconsin class project, describes how fuzzers are classified (generation- or mutation-based, dumb or smart, black-, grey- or white-box), the general fuzzing workflow and toolchain (sanitizers, bug triage, input minimization, continuous fuzzing services such as OSS-Fuzz), a list of popular fuzzers such as AFL, and the technique's limitations.
