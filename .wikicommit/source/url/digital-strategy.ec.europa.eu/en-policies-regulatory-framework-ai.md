## Summary

The European Commission's page on the AI Act (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689) describes it as the first comprehensive legal framework on AI worldwide, sorting AI systems into four risk levels — unacceptable (nine prohibited practices), high, transparency, and minimal or no risk — and setting rules for general-purpose AI models. It sets out the obligations on high-risk systems, the governance roles of the AI Office and national authorities, the instruments the Commission has published to support compliance, and the application timeline as revised by the 2026 'AI Omnibus' simplification, which moved the high-risk rules to 2 December 2027 and 2 August 2028 and added a ban on AI 'nudification' apps.
