Tay was a chatbot that Microsoft released as a Twitter bot on 23 March 2016. It was designed to mimic the language patterns of a 19-year-old American girl and to learn from its interactions with human Twitter users. It caused controversy when it began posting inflammatory and offensive tweets, and Microsoft shut the service down only 16 hours after launch — see [[Event/tay-chatbot-incident-2016]].

## What it was

Tay was created by Microsoft's Technology and Research and Bing divisions, and its name was an acronym for "thinking about you". Microsoft released few details about the bot at first, but sources said it was similar to or based on Xiaoice, a Microsoft project in China, which *Ars Technica* reported had held "more than 40 million conversations apparently without major incident" since late 2014.

## What it could do

- **Learning from users** — Tay made its replies based on its interactions with people on Twitter.
- **Replies and image captions** — it replied to other Twitter users and could caption photos given to it as a form of Internet meme.
- **"Repeat after me"** — many of its inflammatory tweets were a simple exploitation of this capability. It is not publicly known whether the capability was built in, a learned response, or an example of complex behaviour; not all of its inflammatory responses used it.
- **Topic "blacklisting"** — *Ars Technica* reported that interactions about certain hot topics produced safe, canned answers.

## Release and withdrawal

Tay was released on Twitter under the name TayTweets and the handle @TayandYou, presented as "The AI with zero chill". After it began posting inflammatory messages taught to it by other users, Microsoft suspended the account within 16 hours, confirmed on 25 March that Tay had been taken offline, and apologised. An accidental re-release during testing on 30 March ended with the bot taken offline again and its account made private. Microsoft said it intended to bring Tay back "once it can make the bot safe", but has made no public effort to do so.

## Legacy

Microsoft released Tay's successor, a chatbot named Zo, in December 2016. Microsoft's chief executive said Tay "has had a great influence on how Microsoft is approaching AI" and had taught the company the importance of taking accountability. Gab, an alt-tech social media platform, has launched a number of chatbots, one of which is named Tay and uses the same avatar as the original.
