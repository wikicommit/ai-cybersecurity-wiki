"Criminals Use Generative Artificial Intelligence to Facilitate Financial Fraud" is a public service announcement (Alert Number I-120324-PSA) issued by the FBI through its Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3) on December 3, 2024. It warns the public that criminals exploit generative artificial intelligence (AI) to commit fraud on a larger scale and to make their schemes more believable, and gives examples of how they do so so that the public can recognise and scrutinise such content.

## What the announcement warns about

The FBI states that generative AI reduces the time and effort criminals must spend to deceive their targets, and that these tools assist with content creation and can correct for human errors that might otherwise serve as warning signs of fraud. The announcement notes that creating or distributing synthetic content — media generated or manipulated by machine-learning-based techniques — is not inherently illegal, but that such content can be used to facilitate crimes such as fraud and extortion. Because it can be difficult to tell when content is AI-generated, the FBI sets out examples of how criminals may use generative AI in their schemes.

## How criminals use generative AI, by kind of content

| Kind of content | Uses the announcement describes |
| --- | --- |
| AI-generated text | Creating large numbers of fictitious social media profiles to trick victims into sending money; writing messages faster to reach a wider audience; translating to limit grammatical or spelling errors for foreign criminal actors targeting US victims; generating content for fraudulent cryptocurrency and other investment websites; embedding AI-powered chatbots in fraudulent websites to prompt victims to click malicious links |
| AI-generated images | Realistic profile photos for fictitious social media profiles; fraudulent identification documents such as fake driver's licences or law enforcement, government or banking credentials; photos shared privately to convince victims they are speaking to a real person; images of celebrities or social media personas promoting counterfeit products or non-delivery schemes; images of natural disasters or global conflict to elicit donations to fraudulent charities; images used in market manipulation; pornographic photos of a victim used to demand payment in sextortion schemes |
| AI-generated audio ([[DefinedTerm/audio-deepfake\|vocal cloning]]) | Short audio clips in a loved one's voice impersonating a close relative in a crisis and asking for immediate financial help or a ransom; AI-generated audio clips of individuals used to impersonate them and obtain access to bank accounts |
| AI-generated videos | Videos for real-time video chats with supposed company executives, law enforcement or other authority figures; videos for private communications to "prove" an online contact is a real person; fictitious or misleading promotional material for investment fraud |

Across these, the announcement describes AI-generated text being used to appear believable in [[DefinedTerm/social-engineering|social engineering]] (defined in the announcement as obtaining, or attempting to obtain, otherwise secure data by conning an individual into revealing it), spear [[DefinedTerm/phishing|phishing]] (a directed attempt to trick a specific user or group into clicking a malicious link or opening an attachment, using information meant to raise the chance of success) and financial fraud such as romance, investment and other confidence schemes. A non-delivery scam, one of the schemes named, is one in which payment is sent and goods or services are never received or are of lesser quality.

## Advice to the public

The announcement gives these tips:

- Create a secret word or phrase with family to verify their identity.
- Look for subtle imperfections in images and videos, such as distorted hands or feet, unrealistic teeth or eyes, indistinct or irregular faces, unrealistic accessories, inaccurate shadows, watermarks, lag time, voice matching and unrealistic movements.
- Listen closely to tone and word choice to distinguish a legitimate call from a loved one from AI-generated vocal cloning.
- If possible, limit online content of one's image or voice, make social media accounts private and limit followers to people one knows.
- Verify a caller's identity by hanging up, researching the contact details of the bank or organisation they claim to represent, and calling that number directly.
- Never share sensitive information with people met only online or over the phone, and do not send money, gift cards, cryptocurrency or other assets to them.

Victims of financial fraud are asked to file a report with the IC3, including identifying information about the individuals involved, financial transaction details, and a description of the interaction.
