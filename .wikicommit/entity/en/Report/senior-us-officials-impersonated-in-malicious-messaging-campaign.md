"Senior US Officials Impersonated in Malicious Messaging Campaign" is a public service announcement (Alert Number I-051525-PSA) issued by the FBI on May 15, 2025. It warns the public about an ongoing malicious text and voice messaging campaign — described on [[Event/senior-us-officials-impersonation-campaign-2025]] — in which actors impersonate senior US officials, and gives mitigation tips. Its central message is that a message claiming to come from a senior US official should not be assumed to be authentic.

## What the announcement reports

According to the announcement, since April 2025 malicious actors have impersonated senior US officials to target individuals, many of whom are current or former senior US federal or state government officials and their contacts. The actors have sent text messages and AI-generated voice messages claiming to come from a senior US official, in an effort to establish rapport before gaining access to personal accounts. One way they gain such access is by sending a malicious link under the guise of moving the conversation to a separate messaging platform. The FBI warns that access to accounts operated by US officials could be used to target other officials or their associates using the contact information obtained, and that contact information acquired this way could be used to impersonate those contacts to elicit information or funds.

## Terms it explains

| Term | Meaning given in the announcement |
| --- | --- |
| Smishing | The malicious targeting of individuals using SMS or MMS text messaging |
| [[DefinedTerm/voice-phishing\|Vishing]] | The malicious targeting of individuals using voice memos, which may incorporate AI-generated voices |
| Spear [[DefinedTerm/phishing\|phishing]] | Targeting specific individuals or groups by email; smishing and vishing use similar tactics |

The announcement describes these as common criminal tactics. Actors have traditionally used them to move a target to a secondary messaging platform, where they may present malware or links to an actor-controlled site that steals log-in information. For smishing, actors typically use software to generate phone numbers not attributed to a specific phone or subscriber; for vishing, they are more frequently exploiting AI-generated audio to impersonate well-known public figures or personal relations.

## Recommendations

For spotting a fake message, the FBI advises:

- verifying the identity of the person calling or messaging by researching the originating number, organisation or person and independently finding a number to call back;
- carefully examining email addresses, contact details, URLs and spelling, since scammers use slight differences, publicly available photographs, minor alterations to names, or AI-generated voices to masquerade as a known contact;
- looking for subtle imperfections in images and videos, and listening closely to tone and word choice, since AI-generated voice cloning can sound nearly identical to a known contact;
- contacting relevant security officials or the FBI when in doubt, since AI-generated content is often difficult to identify.

To protect against fraud or loss of sensitive information, it advises never sharing sensitive information or an associate's contact information with people met only online or over the phone; verifying a known contact's new platform or number through a previously confirmed channel; independently confirming contact details before sending money or assets; not clicking links or opening attachments before confirming the sender's identity; setting up two-factor or multi-factor authentication and never disabling it or sharing a code; and creating a secret word or phrase with family members.

The announcement refers readers to an earlier FBI public service announcement, [[Report/criminals-use-generative-artificial-intelligence-to-facilitate-financial-fraud]], and to phishing guidance from the Cybersecurity and Infrastructure Security Agency (CISA). Victims are asked to contact their security officials and the FBI, through a local FBI field office or the Internet Crime Complaint Center (IC3).
