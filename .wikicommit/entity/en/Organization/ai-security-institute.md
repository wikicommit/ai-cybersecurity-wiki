The AI Security Institute is the United Kingdom's [[DefinedTerm/artificial-intelligence-safety-institute|artificial intelligence safety institute]], a state-backed body for evaluating and ensuring the safety of advanced AI models. It is part of the Department for Science, Innovation and Technology, and was called the AI Safety Institute until February 2025.

## History

| When | Development |
| --- | --- |
| April 2023 | The UK founded the Frontier AI Taskforce, with an initial budget of £100 million |
| November 2023 | During the [[Event/ai-safety-summit-2023\|AI Safety Summit]], the taskforce evolved into the AI Safety Institute, still led by the same person |
| April 2024 | The UK and the US agreed to collaborate on at least one joint safety test |
| May 2024 | The institute, initially established in London, announced it would open an office in San Francisco, where many AI companies are located — part of a plan to "set new, international standards on AI safety", according to technology minister Michele Donelan. The same month it open-sourced Inspect, an AI safety tool that evaluates model capabilities such as reasoning and degree of autonomy |
| February 2025 | Renamed the AI Security Institute |
| December 2025 | Became network coordinator when the International Network of AI Safety Institutes became the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science |

## Context and reception

The institute was set up as part of prime minister Rishi Sunak's plans to make the UK "the geographical home of global AI safety regulation", with an emphasis on independent safety evaluation. The UK's AI strategy aims to balance safety and innovation: unlike the European Union, which adopted the [[Legislation/artificial-intelligence-act|AI Act]], the UK is reluctant to legislate early, on the view that doing so may lower the sector's growth and that laws might be made obsolete by technological progress.

Observers saw the 2025 change of name as a signal that the institute would not focus on ethical issues such as algorithmic bias or freedom of speech in AI applications.
