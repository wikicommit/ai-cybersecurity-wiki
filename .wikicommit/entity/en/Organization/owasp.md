OWASP, the Open Worldwide Application Security Project (formerly the Open Web Application Security Project), is an online community that publishes open-source information and resources on IoT, system software and web application security. It is led by a non-profit, the OWASP Foundation, and works through industry standards, conferences and workshops; in 2017 it had approximately 13,000 volunteers.

## What it publishes

### OWASP Top Ten

The Top Ten is a listing of the most critical application security risks. It was first published in 2003 and is updated periodically.

| Edition | What the source records |
| --- | --- |
| 2003 | First edition |
| 2004, 2007, 2010, 2013, 2017, 2021 | Subsequent editions |
| 2025 | The latest edition the source lists; it introduced new categories, including software supply chain failures |

Many standards, books, tools and organisations reference the Top Ten, including MITRE, PCI DSS, the Defense Information Systems Agency (DISA-STIG) and the United States Federal Trade Commission. In May 2023 OWASP started the OWASP Gen AI Security Project to expand the scope of the Top Ten list to document the most critical risks associated with large language models.

## History

- **2001** — Mark Curphey started OWASP on 9 September 2001.
- **2003–2011** — Jeff Williams served as volunteer chair from late 2003 until September 2011.
- **2004** — The OWASP Foundation was established in the US as a 501(c)(3) non-profit organisation; it supports OWASP's infrastructure and projects.
- **2011** — OWASP was also registered as a non-profit in Belgium, as OWASP Europe VZW.
- **2023** — In February, an officer of the OWASP Foundation's global board of directors reported that the board had voted to rename the project from the Open Web Application Security Project to its current name, replacing "Web" with "Worldwide". In May, the OWASP Gen AI Security Project was started.
