*Moffatt v. Air Canada*, 2024 BCCRT 149, is a decision of the Civil Resolution Tribunal of British Columbia, issued on February 14, 2024, which held that a company could be held liable for misleading information given to customers by an AI chatbot. Although it was a small-claims case with only CA$880 (US$704) at stake, it received significant international media attention, because it was one of the earliest to hold a company liable for mistakes made by its [[DefinedTerm/ai-agent|AI agent]] and because of the airline's "remarkable" claim that its chatbot was a separate legal entity for whose actions it could not be held responsible.

## The decision

In its submissions, the airline argued that the chatbot was "a separate legal entity that is responsible for its own actions". The tribunal member deciding the case called that submission "remarkable" and rejected it, ruling that because the chatbot formed part of the airline's website, the airline was fully responsible for the information it provided. On that basis the tribunal found the airline liable for negligent misrepresentation and awarded the claimant CA$812 (US$650) — the difference between the price paid and the cost of bereavement fares — plus costs and interest.

## Reactions

The case attracted international attention shortly after the decision was released:

- The American Bar Association called it "a helpful reminder that companies remain liable for the actions of their AI tools".
- An aviation industry expert said: "For passengers, the only lesson is that they cannot fully rely on the information provided by airline chatbots."
- *Business Insider* labelled the chatbot's actions an example of "botshit" and "one example of how the use of AI might worsen companies' customer service".
