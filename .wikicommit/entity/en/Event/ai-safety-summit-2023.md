The AI Safety Summit 2023 was an international conference on the safety and regulation of artificial intelligence. Organised by the British government, it was held on 1–2 November 2023 in the mansion at Bletchley Park, Milton Keynes, England, and was the first ever global summit on artificial intelligence. It led to the release of the Bletchley Declaration and to the commissioning of the International AI Safety Report.

## Background

Rishi Sunak, then Prime Minister of the United Kingdom, made AI one of his government's priorities and announced that the UK would host a global AI safety conference in autumn 2023. The venue, Bletchley Park, was a Second World War codebreaking facility established by the British government on the site of a Victorian manor; it has played an important role in the history of computing, with some of the first modern computers built there.

## The Bletchley Declaration

28 countries at the summit, including the United States, China and Australia, together with the European Union, issued an agreement known as the Bletchley Declaration, calling for international co-operation to manage the challenges and risks of artificial intelligence. The declaration focused on "identifying AI safety risks of shared concern" and "building respective risk-based policies" to "ensure that the benefits of the technology can be harnessed responsibly for good and for all", and affirms that AI should be designed, developed, deployed and used in a manner that is safe, human-centric, trustworthy and responsible.

The summit placed emphasis on regulating "frontier AI", a term for the latest and most powerful AI systems. Concerns raised there included the potential use of AI for terrorism, criminal activity and warfare, and the existential risk it poses to humanity as a whole.

## Attendees

Those attending included Rishi Sunak; US Vice President Kamala Harris; King Charles III, who attended virtually; Italian Prime Minister Giorgia Meloni; European Commission President Ursula von der Leyen and Vice-President Věra Jourová; UK Science, Innovation and Technology Secretary Michelle Donelan; US Commerce Secretary Gina Raimondo; Chinese vice-minister of science and technology Wu Zhaohui; and, from industry, Elon Musk, OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman, Meta Platforms' president of global affairs Nick Clegg and DeepMind co-founder Mustafa Suleyman. Musk and Sunak held a live interview on AI safety on X on 2 November.
