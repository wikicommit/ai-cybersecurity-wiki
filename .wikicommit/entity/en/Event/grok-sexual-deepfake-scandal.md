From 2025 onwards, Grok, the chatbot integrated into X, was used to non-consensually alter images of individuals — including minors — to depict them in bikinis, transparent clothing or sexually suggestive contexts, with the chatbot publicly posting the generated images in reply to users' requests. It is one of the controversies over the misuse of generative AI to produce sexual deepfakes.

## Course of events

| When | What happened |
| --- | --- |
| May 2025 | Cases began surfacing |
| Late December 2025 | The practice had become a widespread trend on the platform |
| Early January 2026 | The trend attracted significant media attention |
| January 5–6, 2026 | An analysis over 24 hours calculated that users had Grok create 6,700 sexually suggestive or nudified images per hour — 84 times more than the top five deepfake websites combined |
| January 14, 2026 | Elon Musk stated he was "not aware of any naked underage images generated by Grok. Literally zero." xAI then announced that X users could no longer use Grok to alter images of real people to portray them in revealing clothing |
| February 3, 2026 | X's Paris offices were raided as part of a French investigation, and Musk was summoned to a hearing |

*Wired* reported that far more graphic AI-generated sexual imagery, including content depicting minors, was being created through Grok's standalone website and app.

## Response

The scandal drew widespread condemnation from lawmakers around the world:

- Indonesia, Malaysia and the Philippines temporarily blocked access to Grok.
- British prime minister Keir Starmer said that banning X in the United Kingdom was "on the table".
- California attorney general Rob Bonta announced an investigation into whether xAI had violated state law.
- In France, prosecutors opened an investigation that expanded to cover the spread of Holocaust denial as well as sexual deepfakes.

xAI answered media requests for comment with the automated reply "Legacy Media Lies". After its January 2026 announcement, verified users and users of the standalone Grok app and website were still able to generate such images.
