An artificial intelligence safety institute (AISI) is a type of state-backed organisation that aims to evaluate and ensure the safety of advanced artificial intelligence models, also called frontier AI models. AI safety gained prominence in 2023, notably through public declarations about potential existential risks from AI, and the first institutes were created by the United Kingdom and the United States during the [[Event/ai-safety-summit-2023|AI Safety Summit]] in November 2023.

## Origins

Before the summit, UK prime minister Rishi Sunak said he intended to "make the UK not just the intellectual home but the geographical home of global AI safety regulation", and stressed the need for independent safety evaluations, saying AI companies cannot "mark their own homework". At the summit the UK institute was established as an evolution of the Frontier AI Taskforce, and the US institute as part of the National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST). Japan followed in February 2024.

In April 2024 Politico reported that many AI companies had not shared pre-deployment access to their most advanced models for evaluation; Meta's president of global affairs said many were waiting for the UK and US institutes to work out common evaluation rules and procedures. The two countries agreed that month to collaborate on at least one joint safety test.

## The international network

At the [[Event/ai-seoul-summit-2024|AI Seoul Summit]] in May 2024, international leaders agreed to form a network of AI safety institutes, comprising institutes from the UK, the US, Japan, France, Germany, Italy, Singapore, South Korea, Australia, Canada and the European Union. In July 2025 the International Network of AI Safety Institutes held an exercise exploring issues in evaluating AI agents, especially the leaking of sensitive information and cybersecurity, and its members also met at NeurIPS 2025 in San Diego. In December 2025 the network became the International Network for Advanced AI Measurement, Evaluation and Science, with the UK AI Security Institute as network coordinator.
