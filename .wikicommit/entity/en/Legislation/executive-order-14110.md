Executive Order 14110, titled "Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence" and sometimes called the "Executive Order on Artificial Intelligence", was a United States presidential directive on artificial intelligence (AI). It was the 126th executive order signed by President Joe Biden, on 30 October 2023 (Federal Register document number 2023-24283). The order defined the administration's policy goals for AI and ordered executive agencies to take actions toward them, and has been described as the most comprehensive piece of governance by the United States regarding AI. President Donald Trump rescinded it within hours of taking office on 20 January 2025.

## Status

The order is no longer in force. Hours after his inauguration as the 47th president on 20 January 2025, Donald Trump rescinded it, listing it among several of Biden's executive orders and actions that he labelled "unpopular, inflationary, illegal, and radical practices".

## Background

The drafting of the order was motivated by the rapid development of generative AI models in the 2020s, including the release of the large language model ChatGPT. It was the third executive order dealing explicitly with AI; the two earlier ones were signed by Donald Trump during his first term.

Concerns raised by experts and commentators about AI development without policy safeguards ranged from future existential risk from advanced AI models to immediate concerns about current technologies' ability to spread misinformation, enable discrimination and undermine national security. In August 2023 the director of the White House Office of Science and Technology Policy indicated that the White House was expediting its work on executive action on AI.

## Policy goals

The order has been characterised as an effort for the United States to capture potential benefits from AI while mitigating its risks. Its policy goals include:

- promoting competition and innovation in the AI industry;
- upholding civil and labour rights and protecting consumers and their privacy from AI-enabled harms;
- specifying federal policies governing the procurement and use of AI;
- developing watermarking systems for AI-generated content and warding off intellectual property theft stemming from the use of generative models;
- maintaining the nation's place as a global leader in AI.

## What it required of federal agencies

| Agency | Requirement under the order |
| --- | --- |
| A number of large federal agencies | Appoint a "chief artificial intelligence officer"; in the days after the order the General Services Administration and the Department of Education were confirmed to have appointed one, and the National Science Foundation elevated an official to the role |
| Department of Homeland Security (DHS) | Develop AI-related security guidelines, including on cybersecurity, and work with private-sector firms in sectors such as energy and other "critical infrastructure" to coordinate responses to AI-enabled security threats |
| National Institute of Standards and Technology (NIST), Department of Commerce | Develop a resource focused on generative AI to supplement the existing AI Risk Management Framework |
| Department of Veterans Affairs | Launch an AI technology competition aimed at reducing occupational burnout among healthcare workers through AI-assisted tools for routine tasks |

## Security relevance

Preventing AI-enabled threats to national security is among the concerns the order addresses. Its security provisions run mainly through the Department of Homeland Security, which was made responsible for AI-related security guidelines, including cybersecurity matters, and for coordinating responses to AI-enabled security threats with critical-infrastructure operators.

## Relation to other measures

Earlier in 2023, the Biden administration had announced a Blueprint for an AI Bill of Rights and secured non-binding AI safety commitments from major technology companies. The order was issued while lawmakers, including Senate Majority Leader Chuck Schumer, were pushing for legislation to regulate AI in the 118th Congress.

According to *Axios*, despite its wide scope the order does not take up several AI policy proposals: it does not create a "licensing regime" for advanced AI models, does not seek to prohibit "high-risk" uses of AI, and does not require technology companies to release information about AI systems' training data and models.

| Party | Position |
| --- | --- |
| *Houston Chronicle* editorial board | Described the order as a "first step toward protecting humanity" |
| Democratic members of Congress | Praised it; Representative Don Beyer, who leads the House AI Caucus, called it a "comprehensive strategy for responsible innovation" while arguing that Congress must pass AI legislation |
| Senator Ted Cruz (Republican) | Criticised the draft as creating "barriers to innovation disguised as safety measures" |

| US Chamber of Commerce, NetChoice, Software and Information Industry Association | Strongly criticised it, arguing it threatens to hinder private-sector innovation |
| Leadership Conference on Civil and Human Rights | Praised it while urging Congress to act |
| American Civil Liberties Union | Praised its provisions against AI-enabled discrimination but voiced concern about the sections on law enforcement and national security |
