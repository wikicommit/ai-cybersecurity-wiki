Executive Order 14179, titled "Removing Barriers to American Leadership in Artificial Intelligence", is a United States executive order signed by Donald Trump, the 47th President of the United States, on 23 January 2025 (Federal Register document number 2025-02172). It aims to begin strengthening U.S. leadership in artificial intelligence (AI), to promote AI development free from ideological bias or social agendas, to establish an action plan to maintain global AI dominance, and to revise or rescind policies that conflict with these goals.

## Background

The order responds to [[Legislation/executive-order-14110|Executive Order 14110]], "Executive Order on Safe, Secure, and Trustworthy Development and Use of Artificial Intelligence", which Joe Biden signed on 30 October 2023. Trump rescinded Executive Order 14110 on his first day in office through the "Initial Rescissions of Harmful Executive Orders and Actions" executive order, and on 23 January 2025 signed Executive Order 14179 as the replacement order covering the development of AI technologies.

## Provisions

- It revokes existing AI policies and directives seen as barriers to U.S. AI innovation.
- It mandates an action plan, to be created within 180 days, to sustain U.S. AI leadership, focusing on human flourishing, economic competitiveness and national security.
- It requires a review of policies, directives and regulations related to Executive Order 14110 to identify actions that may conflict with the new policy goals, and instructs agencies to suspend, revise or rescind actions under the previous order that may be inconsistent with the new policy.
- The Office of Management and Budget (OMB) must revise memoranda M-24-10 and M-24-18 within 60 days to align them with the new policy.
- The order states that it creates no new enforceable rights or benefits and is to be implemented within existing law and appropriations.

## Implementation and public input

On behalf of the Office of Science and Technology Policy (OSTP), the Networking and Information Technology Research and Development (NITRD) program requested public input on the development of an AI Action Plan by 15 March. Over 10,000 public comments were submitted in response.

Among them, OpenAI proposed a five-point strategy focused on regulatory preemption, export controls, copyright protections, infrastructure investment and government adoption, to ensure AI innovation, promote democratic AI globally and protect national security; it emphasised the ability to learn from copyrighted material to maintain America's lead against China's state-controlled AI efforts such as DeepSeek. Google advocated a three-pronged plan: investing in domestic AI development through energy infrastructure reform, balanced export controls, continued research funding and coherent federal policies, while modernising government AI adoption and promoting innovation-friendly approaches internationally. Both companies urged the White House to oppose foreign copyright and transparency obligations, such as the option the UK Government preferred in its Copyright and AI consultation.
