Executive Order 14365, "Ensuring a National Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence", is a United States executive order signed by President Donald Trump in December 2025 that seeks to preempt state regulation of artificial intelligence.

## What it directs

The order directs federal agencies to:

- develop a unified national approach to AI policy;
- evaluate state AI laws for potential conflicts;
- challenge those laws through legal action; and
- condition certain federal funding on state compliance.

It exempts state laws related to child safety, data center infrastructure and state government procurement.

## Background

Several US states have enacted AI laws, some already in effect (including in California) and others coming into effect in 2026 and 2027. In 2025 and 2026 the Trump administration cited the patchwork nature of state legislation as a motivation for its push for unified national legislation on AI; it has criticized state lawmakers, threatened to sue states and sent letters discouraging them from regulating AI companies and products, while some states have continued to propose and enact such laws.

Before the order, in March 2025, OpenAI proposed that the administration preempt pending state AI laws with federal law, and Meta, Google, IBM and Andreessen Horowitz also pressed for national rules that would rein in state laws, especially in California. In May 2025, House Republicans inserted a clause banning state AI laws for 10 years into a tax and spending bill; it was opposed by more than 100 nonprofit organizations, elected officials, public policy experts and others, and the Senate voted 99–1 to defeat it.

## Follow-up

In March 2026 the White House published "A National Legislative Policy Framework for Artificial Intelligence: Legislative Recommendations", a set of recommendations asking Congress to act in various ways to implement the administration's AI policy strategy through federal legislation.
