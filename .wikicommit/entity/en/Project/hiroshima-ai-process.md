The Hiroshima AI Process is an international initiative launched by the Group of Seven (G7) in 2023 to coordinate approaches to the governance of advanced and generative artificial intelligence. It was established at the 49th G7 summit in Hiroshima, Japan, in May 2023, as a forum for international discussion of the risks, standards and policy responses related to rapidly developing AI technologies. Its membership is the G7 member states and partners, and its scope is global.

## Origins

The process was initiated amid increasing international concern about the rapid development of generative AI systems and the lack of coordinated global regulatory approaches. At the 2023 G7 summit in Hiroshima, leaders agreed to establish a ministerial-level process to examine the governance challenges posed by advanced AI and to develop shared approaches to standards and risk management.

## How it works and what it aims at

The Hiroshima AI Process functions as an ongoing framework for dialogue among G7 members and partners on the safe and trustworthy development of advanced AI systems. Policy analysts have described its aims as promoting international coordination, encouraging transparency, and developing shared governance principles for emerging AI technologies.

## What it has produced

In October 2023, G7 leaders agreed, under the process, on international guiding principles and a voluntary code of conduct for organisations developing advanced AI systems.

## Place among other governance efforts

The initiative emerged alongside other international efforts on AI governance, including work associated with the Organisation for Economic Co-operation and Development (OECD) and policy initiatives in the European Union. It has been described as part of a wider competition among major economies to shape global AI governance and to harmonise regulatory approaches.
